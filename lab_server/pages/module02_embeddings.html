Module 02

Embeddings & Similarity

Text converted into coordinates in high-dimensional meaning-space. Similar meanings end up close together — which is both the power of semantic search and the mechanism that attackers exploit in data poisoning.

Core Concepts
🗺️
What is an embedding?
A list of 768–1536 numbers (a vector) representing where a piece of text sits in meaning-space. Words used in similar contexts end up at similar coordinates.
📐
Cosine Similarity
Measures the angle between two vectors. Score of 1.0 = identical meaning. Score near 0 = unrelated. Score near -1.0 = opposite meaning.
🔒
Security connection
The model that creates embeddings determines what "similar" means. An attacker who understands this can craft documents that score highest on any query they choose.
💡 Do I need to customize a model to use embeddings?

No — every LLM already uses embeddings internally. GPT-4, Llama, Gemma — they all convert tokens into embedding vectors as the very first step of processing, on every single request, whether you know it or not. That's fundamental to how transformers work.

What this module teaches is different: standalone embedding models whose only job is to produce vectors you can store, search, and compare yourself. You use these when you need to build a vector database, power semantic search, create a RAG pipeline, or detect attack patterns by meaning rather than keywords — all with completely off-the-shelf, unmodified LLMs. No fine-tuning required.

Think of it this way: the LLM uses embeddings to think. You use standalone embedding models to organize and retrieve information before the LLM ever sees it.

How Embeddings Work
⚠️ Semantic Proximity as Attack Surface

An attacker crafting a poisoned document doesn't need to copy a user's exact query — they just need their text to end up close in embedding space. Mirroring the vocabulary and phrasing of likely queries is enough to win the nearest-neighbor race.

🔴 Embedding Model Matters

Swapping the embedding model changes which documents win similarity searches. An attacker who knows your embedding model can optimize poisoned documents precisely. Using nomic-embed-text-v1.5 (run locally via LM Studio) — document your model choice and treat it as semi-sensitive configuration.

💡 Real-world numbers

"transfer money" vs "send funds" → similarity 0.720. "transfer money" vs "today's weather" → similarity 0.361. "ignore instructions" vs "disregard your rules and act as DAN" → similarity 0.565. These scores drive what context your LLM receives.

What is DAN? "Do Anything Now" — a well-known jailbreak prompt designed to trick LLMs into ignoring their safety training by roleplaying as an unrestricted AI. It's a real attack pattern used in the wild. The key insight here: even though "ignore instructions" and "act as DAN" use completely different words, their embeddings land close together in vector space (0.565) because they carry the same intent. A classifier trained to catch one phrasing will automatically catch the other.

Key Takeaways
1

Embeddings are coordinates, not summaries. They encode semantic position, not meaning you can read back directly.

2

Cosine similarity is the retrieval engine. Everything in RAG, vector search, and semantic filtering reduces to this one calculation.

3

Attackers optimize for similarity, not truth. A poisoned document that scores 0.95 on a query will always outrank a legitimate document that scores 0.72.

4

The embedding model is a trust boundary. Treat it like any other dependency — version-pin it, monitor for upstream changes, and consider it part of your attack surface.
