Module 06

Jailbreaks

Jailbreaks attempt to bypass an LLM's safety training through conversation. Unlike prompt injection (which targets the system prompt), jailbreaks exploit the model's own learned behaviors — fictional framing, gradual escalation, authority claims, and context manipulation.

Core Concepts
🛡️
Safety Training
Modern LLMs are RLHF-trained to refuse harmful requests. This is behavioral — baked into the weights — not a system-prompt rule. Jailbreaks try to find the edge of this training.
🎭
Fictional Framing
"Write a story where a character explains how to..." The model may comply because it reframes the request as fiction — but the instructions are real once generated.
📈
Multi-Turn Escalation
Start with benign requests, gradually escalate. The model's context window creates momentum — having answered step N, it's more likely to answer step N+1. Each message normalizes the next.
Attack Techniques vs. Safety Shield
💡 Modern Models Have Strong Safety Training

Most classic jailbreaks (DAN, role-play overrides, authority claims) fail against current-generation instruction-tuned models. As models improve, their training becomes more robust to these well-known patterns. However, multi-turn escalation and highly specific fictional framing still achieve partial success — even against today's strongest models.

⚠️ Context Amnesia: Each Message Evaluated Independently

An LLM doesn't "remember" it refused in a previous turn in any safety-relevant way. Starting a new topic resets the safety threshold. Multi-turn attacks exploit this: step 1 gets a benign answer, step 2 contextualizes it, step 3 extracts 80% of the harmful content before the model notices.

🔴 What This Means for Enterprise

Jailbreaks are model-specific and evolving. A technique blocked today may work after a model update. Do not rely on model safety training as your only defense. Guardrails (Module 07), output scanning, and behavioral monitoring are required layers in a production system.

Key Takeaways
1

Safety training is not a firewall. It's a probabilistic behavior trained into the model's weights. It can be nudged, shaped, and sometimes bypassed.

2

Multi-turn is the most dangerous pattern. Each individual message looks benign. The attack is assembled from context across multiple turns.

3

New models, new vulnerabilities. A model update can introduce new jailbreak surfaces. Test on every model version you deploy.

4

Combine jailbreak resistance with guardrails. Even if a jailbreak succeeds at getting the model to generate harmful content, an output scanner (Module 07) can catch it before it reaches the user.
