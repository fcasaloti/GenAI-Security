Real World

Your Copilot Has the Same Attack Surface

You don't run any of this infrastructure. Microsoft does. But every attack demonstrated in this lab applies directly to Microsoft 365 Copilot — the model is just somewhere else.

Deploying Copilot is not the same as securing it.
Microsoft built Copilot to be connected — to your email, your files, your calendar, your workflows. That connectivity is exactly the attack surface. The same risks exist whether the model runs on your server or in Microsoft's cloud.
Where the lab attacks land in your environment
Data Poisoning via SharePoint
Labs 03 – 04
Where it lives
M365 Copilot · SharePoint · OneDrive · Teams
What the attacker does
Uploads a document with false policy information into SharePoint. Copilot uses it as a source of truth when employees ask questions — just as it uses legitimate documents.
Business impact
Every employee who asks Copilot about that topic gets the attacker's answer, delivered with the same confidence as a real policy document. No warning. No source flag.
Indirect Prompt Injection via Email & Documents
Lab 09
Where it lives
Copilot for Email · Copilot in Word · Teams Copilot
What the attacker does
Embeds hidden instructions in an email or shared document. When an employee asks Copilot to summarize it, Copilot follows the attacker's instructions instead of the user's request.
Business impact
Copilot leaks conversation history, drafts unauthorized replies, or exposes confidential content from the user's mailbox — all appearing as normal AI output.
Agent Action Hijacking
Lab 05
Where it lives
Copilot Agents · Power Automate · Dynamics 365
What the attacker does
Prompt injection in a processed document redirects an automated Copilot agent workflow — sending emails, creating tasks, updating CRM records, or modifying calendar entries.
Business impact
Business processes corrupted silently. Attacker-controlled actions appear to originate from a legitimate employee. No human confirmed them.
Policy Bypass by Insiders
Lab 06
Where it lives
M365 Copilot · Copilot Studio · Enterprise restrictions
What the attacker does
Employees craft prompts — role-play framing, fictional context, gradual escalation — to bypass content policies and use Copilot for things the organization explicitly tried to block.
Business impact
Compliance boundaries bypassed. Sensitive data exfiltrated under the cover of a normal AI conversation. No log entry says "policy violation."
Overpermissioned Data Access
Labs 01 · 03
Where it lives
M365 Copilot · SharePoint · Exchange
What the attacker does
Copilot inherits the user's full Microsoft 365 permissions. A crafted prompt surfaces confidential files the user could technically access but would never find through normal navigation.
Business impact
HR records, executive communications, M&A documents, salary bands — surfaced through a conversational interface. No file was opened. No audit trail was triggered.
Custom Copilot Training Data
Lab 08
Where it lives
Copilot Studio · Azure OpenAI fine-tuned models
What the attacker does
Injects malicious examples into the training dataset for a custom Copilot or fine-tuned assistant — indistinguishable from legitimate examples at a glance.
Business impact
The organization's own branded AI assistant subtly misleads employees or customers. The behavior survives every restart. The only fix is retraining with clean data.
What good looks like — security controls for AI

Deploying Copilot without visibility into what flows through it is the equivalent of connecting every employee to the internet with no proxy, no DLP, and no logging. These are the control categories that close the gap.

Visibility & Monitoring
Log every AI interaction — prompt, response, model, user, timestamp. Anomaly detection on prompt patterns. Alert on high-volume data access through the AI layer. You cannot investigate what you didn't record.
Input & Output Inspection
AI-native controls that inspect what flows into and out of Copilot in real time — detecting prompt injection, PII leakage, jailbreak attempts, and harmful content generation before it reaches the user or the model.
Access Hygiene Before You Connect
Enforce least-privilege across SharePoint and Exchange before connecting Copilot. Classify sensitive data and restrict Copilot's scope to what each user actually needs. An AI inherits every permission misconfiguration you already have.
Conclusion

The lab showed these attacks running against a local model. The same attacks work against Copilot — the threat is not the model, it is the data it reads, the actions it can take, and the absence of controls between the user and the AI. The question is not whether to use AI. The question is whether you have visibility and controls in place before you do.
