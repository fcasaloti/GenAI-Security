MODULE 01

Tokenization

AI models do not read words. They read small fragments called tokens. This small detail has big security consequences — it is the root cause of filter bypass attacks and cost-amplification attacks.

Key Concepts
✂️
What is a token?
Before the AI reads anything, your text is cut into small pieces — tokens. A token is roughly a word or part of a word. The AI works with these pieces, not with the original text. Security filters that check your text may miss attacks that slip through the cracks between pieces.
📐
Common words, fewer pieces
Common words get one token. Rare or unusual words get split into several. This means text with unusual characters can balloon in size — costing more and consuming more of the AI's attention than it looks like it should.
🔢
Why it matters for security
Every piece of text the AI processes — your instructions, the user's message, results from tools — gets tokenized the same way. The AI has no built-in way to tell which tokens came from a trusted source and which came from an attacker.

Security Implications
⚠️ Filter Bypass

A content filter that searches for a banned word in plain text can be tricked by an attacker who splits that word across token boundaries using unusual spacing or characters. The word looks different to the filter but means the same thing to the AI. Filters must work at multiple layers — not just on the raw text.

🔴 Cost Inflation Attack

Unusual characters and repeated rare sequences create far more tokens per character than normal text. An attacker can craft a short-looking message that consumes enormous AI resources and inflates your API bill — a low-effort, high-impact denial-of-service. Always set input length limits before the AI ever sees the text.

💡 The root of most AI attacks

Every prompt injection attack in modules 02–06 works for the same reason: the AI cannot tell the difference between your instructions and an attacker's instructions — they all look the same at the token level. Tokenization is where that equivalence begins.

Key Takeaways
1
Short text can be expensive. Text with unusual characters can generate many more AI processing units (tokens) than it appears to. Ignoring this leads to runaway costs and capacity issues.
2
Content filters can be fooled by fragmentation. An attacker can split a banned phrase across fragment boundaries so that a simple text search misses it. Layered, redundant checks are essential.
3
The AI treats all text the same. Your security instructions and an attacker's injected commands look identical to the AI. There is no built-in trust hierarchy. This is why all subsequent attacks in this lab work.
4
Validate input before the AI ever sees it. Length limits, allowed character sets, and rate limiting are your first line of defense — and they must run before the AI processes anything.
